Your voice can make a difference

What patient and family advisors do

Advisors work with hospital staff to improve safety, quality, communication, and the overall experience of care.

💬

Make care safer and better

Help improve patient safety, the quality of care, and the experiences of patients, families, and staff.

📄

Shape how families are involved

Give advice on the best ways to support families and caregivers and make it easier for families to take part in care.

♡

Share experiences to help others

Talk with hospital groups to improve their support of patient- and family-centered care.

◎

Speak up for the whole community

Make sure different voices are heard and that everyone's opinions are respected.

Questions applicants may have

Frequently asked questions

A strong recruitment page should answer practical questions before asking someone to complete a formal application.

Do I have to know which council is right for me?

No. The interest form can include an “I’m not sure” option so a PFAC coordinator can help match your experience and interests.

What is the time commitment?

The current UNC Hospitals page says advisors generally commit to a two-year term. The final site should also state the actual meeting frequency, typical meeting length, and whether each council meets in person, virtually, or both.

When would volunteer requirements be completed?

After an applicant speaks with the PFAC team and a possible placement is identified, the person may need to complete a background check, vaccination documentation, and UNC Hospitals volunteer training.

What information should not be entered?

An initial interest form should not ask for a medical record number or detailed clinical history. It should collect only the information needed to contact the person and discuss possible service.

An essential alternate path

Prefer to talk with someone?

The final page should offer a real telephone number, email address, and callback option for people who cannot or do not want to complete an online form.
